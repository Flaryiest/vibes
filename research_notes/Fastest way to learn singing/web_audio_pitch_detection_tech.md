# Web audio pitch detection tech for a React singing-practice app (state as of 2026-11-13)

Notes on method: package versions, release dates, licences and weekly downloads were read directly from `registry.npmjs.org` / `api.npmjs.org` on 2026-11-13; GitHub stars, last-push dates and licences from `api.github.com` on the same day; several open-source apps were inspected at source level (raw files on GitHub). Where a claim comes only from a search-result summary or a low-authority page, it is marked "secondary". Anything I could not source is in a Gaps list, not in Cited Findings.

## 1. Pitch detection algorithms for the singing voice (accuracy, octave errors, latency, CPU, low fundamentals 65-130 Hz; window size and latency)

### Takeaway
For a live pitch cursor on a bass/baritone voice, a time-domain periodicity method (MPM or YIN) on a 2048-4096-sample window at 44.1/48 kHz is still the practical choice: it is cheap, has no model download, and its latency is set only by the window (about 43-93 ms of audio). The best-scoring current neural tracker, SwiftF0 (MIT, 135 KB ONNX, range 46.875-2093.75 Hz), needs 176 ms of lookahead when streaming, so it fits post-take analysis and octave cross-checking better than the live display.

### Cited Findings

**Independent-ish benchmark (19 trackers, 10 corpora, degraded audio)**
- The `lars76/pitch-benchmark` suite (v2) scores pitch F1 at 50 cents, averaged over eight degraded recording conditions (background sound, reverberation, microphone filtering) and ten corpora; a "hit" is a frame that is voiced and within 50 cents. - Speed on one CPU core (× real time): SwiftF0 179.6, RMVPE 13.6, FCPE 27.8, CREPE 0.4, TorchCREPE 0.4, PESTO 20.0, SHS 1080, Praat 525, SWIPE 50.3, SPICE 86.2, BasicPitch 59.7, pYIN 9.6. CREPE (full) is slower than real time on one core. — [pitch-benchmark README](https://github.com/lars76/pitch-benchmark)
- Clean (unmodified) audio pitch F1: SwiftF0 0.949, RMVPE 0.943, FCPE 0.935, Praat 0.925, CREPE 0.914, SHS 0.894, PESTO 0.868, SWIPE 0.855, SPICE 0.767, BasicPitch 0.711, pYIN 0.678. — [BENCHMARK.md](https://github.com/lars76/pitch-benchmark/blob/main/BENCHMARK.md)

- Voicing F1: PESTO 0.850, SwiftF0 0.844, RMVPE 0.837, Praat 0.815, FCPE 0.812, CREPE 0.781, SPICE 0.751, pYIN 0.656 (lowest). pYIN's pitch accuracy within 50 cents among voiced frames is 82.4% (mid-table), so its low overall rank comes largely from voicing decisions and noise (scene factor effect −0.270). — [BENCHMARK.md](https://github.com/lars76/pitch-benchmark/blob/main/BENCHMARK.md)
- On the one solo-singing corpus (Vocadito): RMVPE 0.862, SwiftF0 0.847, FCPE 0.786, CREPE 0.781, pYIN 0.653. — [BENCHMARK.md](https://github.com/lars76/pitch-benchmark/blob/main/BENCHMARK.md)
- Benchmark caveats: six of ten corpora are speech with laryngograph/EGG ground truth, plus one synthetic speech set; only Vocadito is solo vocals; search ranges were 65-400 Hz for most corpora and 80-1000 Hz for Vocadito; YIN and MPM are not among the 19 trackers; no per-pitch-range or male/female breakdown is published. The benchmark author is also SwiftF0's author. — [BENCHMARK.md](https://github.com/lars76/pitch-benchmark/blob/main/BENCHMARK.md); [swift-f0 repo](https://github.com/lars76/swift-f0)
- A separate model card reports clean PTDB-TUG frame error (lower is better): FCPE 2.77, RMVPE 3.09, PESTO 5.58, CREPE full 6.43, pyin 7.72 (single condition, from a model-packaging project; indicative only). — [Scicom-intl/rmvpe-onnx](https://huggingface.co/Scicom-intl/rmvpe-onnx)

**SwiftF0**
- Current release 0.3.0 (2026-09-25); 0.2.0 (2026-09-19) replaced the paper's network with a 14,386-parameter model in a 135 KB file. MIT. Input 16 kHz, one estimate per 16 ms frame, range 46.875-2093.75 Hz (about F♯1-C7), per-frame voicing confidence (≥0.5 = voiced), `fmin`/`fmax` band limiting. — [swift-f0 README](https://github.com/lars76/swift-f0); [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/lars76/swift-f0/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)
- Streaming: "A frame is final once 176 ms of audio after it have arrived"; about 16× real time with 20 ms chunks, 60× with 100 ms chunks. No level normalisation: input with peak below about −35 dBFS should be scaled first. — [swift-f0 README](https://github.com/lars76/swift-f0)
- The original paper (v0.1) described 95,842 parameters, 42× faster than CREPE on CPU, and 91.80% harmonic mean at 10 dB SNR. These are the authors' own numbers and describe the older network. — [arXiv 2508.18440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18440)

**CREPE**
- Input is a 1024-sample excerpt at 16 kHz (64 ms); output is 360 bins of 20 cents covering C1 (32.70 Hz) to B7 (1975.5 Hz). Raw pitch accuracy at 50 cents: RWC-synth CREPE 0.999 vs pYIN 0.990 vs SWIPE 0.963; MDB-stem-synth 0.967 vs 0.919 vs 0.925; at 10 cents on MDB-stem-synth 0.909 vs 0.826 vs 0.816. — [CREPE paper (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.06182)
- In brown noise (energy concentrated at low frequencies) "pYIN's performance is almost unaffected", which the authors attribute to YIN's robustness there. — [CREPE paper (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.06182)
- Reference implementation: MIT, default 10 ms step, optional Viterbi smoothing, model capacities tiny/small/medium/large/full; last push 2024-08-19. — [marl/crepe](https://github.com/marl/crepe)

**PESTO, FCPE**
- PESTO (TISMIR 2025 version) claims latency under 10 ms via a streamable VQT with cached convolutions; secondary coverage of the paper gives raw pitch accuracy MIR-1K 96.1% vs CREPE 97.8% and MDB 95.5% vs 96.6%. Licence LGPL-3.0; last push 2025-10-15. — [arXiv 2508.01488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01488); [SonyCSLParis/pesto](https://github.com/SonyCSLParis/pesto)
- PESTO parameter count is given as about 130K in summaries of the 2025 paper, and as "under 30k" for the ISMIR 2023 version by a third party. — [arXiv 2508.01488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01488); contradicted by [@audio/neural-pitch README](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@audio/neural-pitch)
- FCPE: MIT, last push 2025-10-14, 214 stars. — [CNChTu/FCPE](https://github.com/CNChTu/FCPE)

**Classical methods: qualitative notes and window rule**
- pitchfinder's maintainer notes: YIN "best balance of accuracy and speed... Occasionally provides values that are wildly incorrect"; McLeod "also works well. Some have reported better performance on lower frequencies"; AMDF "slow and only accurate to around +/- 2%"; Dynamic Wavelet "very fast, but struggles to identify lower frequencies". — [pitchfinder README](https://github.com/peterkhayes/pitchfinder)
- The audiojs MPM package recommends MPM for "vibrato tracking, small hop sizes, singing voice where YIN occasionally double-triggers" and warns against it for "highly noisy signals"; cost is O(N²/4), the same asymptotic cost as YIN (their direct implementation). — [@audio/pitch-mcleod](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@audio/pitch-mcleod)
- Window rule: a YIN implementation comment says buffer length "equals twice longest period"; practitioners state the window "should be set to be at least twice the longest period"; TarsosDSP's MPM documents minimum detectable frequency as 2 × sampleRate / bufferSize (3072 samples at 44.1 kHz → 29 Hz). — [Audacity/Nyquist yin.c](https://sources.debian.org/src/audacity/2.0.6-2/lib-src/libnyquist/nyquist/nyqsrc/yin.c); [KVR forum](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=4204775); [TarsosDSP McLeodPitchMethod](https://0110.be/releases/TarsosDSP/TarsosDSP-latest/TarsosDSP-latest-Documentation/be/tarsos/dsp/pitch/McLeodPitchMethod.html)
- UltraStar Deluxe analyses the newest 4096 samples with a (circular) AMDF over 49 semitones C2 (65.41 Hz)-C6, and its source comments that at about 65 Hz a 4096 buffer at 44.1/48 kHz gives "only 6 (or 5)" comparisons and "this might be too few samples -> use a bigger buffer-size". — [USDX URecord.pas](https://github.com/UltraStar-Deluxe/USDX/blob/master/src/base/URecord.pas)
- Existing tools often set a floor that cuts off bass notes: Nightingale rejects anything below 80 Hz (`MIN_PITCH_HZ = 80`) with a 2048-sample window. — [Nightingale constants.ts](https://github.com/rzru/nightingale/blob/master/client/src/features/playback/lib/pitch/constants.ts)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic from the "2 × longest period" rule (fmin = 2·sr/N): 1024 samples → 86 Hz at 44.1 kHz, 94 Hz at 48 kHz (cannot reach C2-F2); 2048 → 43 / 47 Hz; 4096 → 21.5 / 23.4 Hz. Periods of C2 (65.41 Hz) inside the window: 1024@48k = 1.4; 2048@48k = 2.8; 2048@44.1k = 3.0; 3072@48k = 4.2; 4096@48k = 5.6; 4096@44.1k = 6.1.
- Window duration: 2048@48k = 42.7 ms; 2048@44.1k = 46.4 ms; 3072@48k = 64 ms; 4096@48k = 85.3 ms; 4096@44.1k = 92.9 ms. A new note is fully inside the window only after one window length, and a steady pitch change is reflected after roughly half a window, so algorithmic lag is about 21-43 ms (2048) or 43-93 ms (4096), before adding hop interval, input-device latency and one display frame.
- Practical setting for this user: 2048 samples is the minimum that covers 65 Hz and leaves under three periods at C2 at 48 kHz; 3072-4096 gives more stable low notes at the cost of roughly 20-45 ms more lag. A sensible design is a 2048 window for the live cursor plus a 4096 window (or SwiftF0) for scoring, with a search band of about 55-700 Hz for a bass/baritone.
- Spectral methods (HPS and relatives) are a poor fit below 100 Hz: FFT bin spacing at 4096@48k is 11.7 Hz while a semitone at 65 Hz is 3.9 Hz, so they need long windows plus interpolation. The nearest benchmarked relative (SHS) has both octave-up and octave-down errors above 1.4.
- The benchmark's octave data suggest the classic risk for low voices differs by family: autocorrelation-style trackers (Praat, RAPT, pYIN) mostly err an octave down; PESTO and CREPE mostly err an octave up. A bass singer's octave-up errors are the more damaging case for a trainer, which argues against PESTO as a sole detector.
- pYIN's HMM/Viterbi stage needs future context, so it is better suited to post-take analysis than to a live cursor (design reasoning; not measured here).
- SwiftF0's 176 ms lookahead makes it unsuitable for the live cursor; it is a strong candidate for (a) re-analysing the recorded take for the score and (b) resolving octave ambiguity in the live MPM track after the fact.

### Gaps
- No published head-to-head of YIN vs MPM vs neural trackers restricted to 65-130 Hz male singing was found; the benchmark has no per-pitch-range breakdown and omits YIN and MPM.
- Octave-error units in BENCHMARK.md were not confirmed.
- SPICE details (licence, window, range) and RMVPE's licence were not checked; no evidence was found of newer lightweight models beyond SwiftF0, PESTO, FCPE and RMVPE.
- The original YIN (2002), pYIN (2014) and MPM (2005) papers were not re-read in this session; their error figures are not quoted here.

## 2. JavaScript/WASM libraries available now

### Takeaway
`pitchy` (MPM, permissive, tiny, pure JS) is the safe default and is what a 1.5k-star karaoke app ships; the copyleft options (pitchfinder GPL-3, aubio GPL-3, essentia.js AGPL-3) and the stale ML ports (tfjs last released 2024, ml5 v1 has no pitch module, basic-pitch-ts untouched since 2023) should be avoided or used knowingly. For neural detection the maintained runtime is `onnxruntime-web` with the SwiftF0 ONNX file; there is no npm package for it, but the MIT demo source is a working reference.

### Inferences
- Licence filter for "permissive only": pitchy, @audio/pitch, onnxruntime-web + SwiftF0, meyda, basic-pitch and the Rust `pitch-detection` crate pass; pitchfinder (GPL), aubiojs (GPL core), essentia.js (AGPL) and PESTO (LGPL weights/code) do not. For a private single-user app copyleft has no practical effect, but it matters if the app is ever published.
- pitchy has no DOM or Web Audio dependency (it takes a typed array), so it can run on the main thread, in a Worker, or bundled into an AudioWorklet module; the README does not state this explicitly.
- pitchy's low release cadence reflects a finished, small library, and it is still in production use (Nightingale). `@audio/pitch` is more actively released and adds pYIN, but is four months old with a few hundred weekly downloads, so it carries more adoption risk for an AI-assisted build.
- CREPE in the browser means TensorFlow.js (stagnant) or a self-converted ONNX model; given CREPE's 0.4× real-time speed for the full model and SwiftF0's better scores at 135 KB, CREPE is no longer the obvious neural choice.

### Gaps
- Bundle sizes after minification/gzip were not measured (only npm unpacked sizes and raw file sizes).
- Meyda's lack of a fundamental-frequency extractor is from prior knowledge, not verified in this session.
- No accuracy reports specific to the JS ports (pitchy vs pitchfinder vs aubiojs) were found.
- SPICE and CREPE TF.js ports: no maintained npm package was identified; this was not searched exhaustively.

## 3. Audio pipeline design (getUserMedia constraints, AudioWorklet vs AnalyserNode vs ScriptProcessorNode, sample rate, off-main-thread detection, React integration)

### Takeaway
Request the mic with `echoCancellation`, `noiseSuppression` and `autoGainControl` set to plain `false`, then read back `track.getSettings()` because browsers (notably Android Chrome and iOS Safari) do not always honour them. Capture in an AudioWorklet that fills a ring buffer and hands fixed chunks to a Worker for detection; keep the 30-60 Hz pitch stream out of React state and draw to a canvas from refs.

### Cited Findings
- Audio-track constraints defined by the platform: `autoGainControl`, `channelCount`, `echoCancellation` (boolean or string type), `latency`, `noiseSuppression`, `sampleRate`, `sampleSize`; `getConstraints()` returns what was requested and `getSettings()` what was applied. — [MDN MediaTrackConstraints](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaTrackConstraints)
- AudioWorklet `process()` "is called synchronously from the audio rendering thread, once for each block of audio"; blocks are "always 128 frames long" today, but "you must always check the size of the sample array rather than assuming a particular size"; an uncaught error makes the node output silence for the rest of its life. — [MDN AudioWorkletProcessor.process](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioWorkletProcessor/process)
- Reference pipeline (SwiftF0 demo): `getUserMedia({audio: {channelCount: 1, echoCancellation: false, noiseSuppression: false, autoGainControl: true}})`; tries `new AudioContext({sampleRate: 16000})` and falls back to the default rate with a windowed-sinc resampler when the browser refuses; the worklet accumulates into a `Float32Array(1600)` and transfers each full chunk with `postMessage(buffer, [buffer.buffer])`; inference runs in a dedicated worker. — [live.ts](https://github.com/swift-f0/swift-f0.github.io/blob/main/src/live.ts); [capture-worklet.js](https://github.com/swift-f0/swift-f0.github.io/blob/main/src/capture-worklet.js)
- Reference pipeline (Nightingale): ring buffer of 2 × window, detection tick every 33 ms ("~30 Hz pitch updates: more than enough for vocal pitch tracking"), with a separate canvas-based pitch graph component. — [use-mic-pitch.ts](https://github.com/rzru/nightingale/blob/master/client/src/features/microphone/hooks/use-mic-pitch.ts); [pitch-graph-canvas.ts](https://github.com/rzru/nightingale/blob/master/client/src/features/playback/components/pitch-graph-canvas.ts)
- Safari default: an Apple engineer states (June 2026) that "WebRTC is implemented by WebKit for Safari, which enables `echoCancellation` by default. Try explicitly setting it to false in your getUserMedia constraints", and that with echo cancellation active other apps recording at the same time can see significantly reduced input volume. — [Apple developer forum: Microphone Gain on Safari/MacOS](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/832452)
- Safari side effects of `echoCancellation: false`: Safari 26 on macOS "produces stereo recording even if I set channelCount: 1" (October 2025, unanswered); a WebKit bug is titled "Disabling echoCancellation in getUserMedia request results in left channel audio only"; on Safari 18.5/macOS 15.5 a reporter saw audio "ducked for a 2-3 seconds" the first time capture started with it off; another bug is titled "First audio stream will be silence when re-create audio with echoCancellation: false". — [Apple developer forum thread 804765](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/804765); [WebKit bug 281978](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=281978); [WebKit bug 294623](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=294623); [WebKit bug 267676](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=267676)
- Android Chrome (secondary, from a search summary of a WebRTC discussion titled "How to avoid loudness reduction for sustained tones?"): `applyConstraints` with `echoCancellation: {exact: false}` worked in Chrome 124 for Android but threw `OverconstrainedError` in Chrome 126, and on Chrome 119 setting `noiseSuppression`/`autoGainControl` to false made "no discernible difference" on some devices. — [discuss-webrtc thread](https://groups.google.com/g/discuss-webrtc/c/PDZYMzAI-_Y)
- Chrome's Chrome 64-era notes say that when echo cancellation is not enabled, Chrome does not touch hardware noise suppression (read via a search summary of a translated page). — [Chrome: disabling hardware noise suppression](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/disabling-hardware-noise-suppression)
- Zustand documents "transient updates (for often occurring state-changes)": `subscribe` lets a component "bind to a state-portion without forcing re-render on changes". — [zustand README](https://github.com/pmndrs/zustand#transient-updates-for-often-occurring-state-changes)
- Tone.js exposes the same trade-off for scheduling: `context.updateInterval + context.lookAhead` "gives you the total latency between scheduling an event and hearing it". — [Tone.js Context docs](https://tonejs.github.io/docs/14.7.77/Context)

### Inferences
- Recommended capture graph: `MediaStreamAudioSourceNode → AudioWorkletNode` (no connection to `destination`, to avoid monitoring feedback). The worklet copies each 128-frame block into a ring buffer and every hop (for example 512 or 1024 frames) posts a transferable chunk to a Worker that runs pitchy on the latest 2048-4096 samples and posts back `{t, hz, clarity, rms}`. Running pitchy inside the worklet itself is possible but puts FFT work on the audio thread for no benefit.
- `AnalyserNode.getFloatTimeDomainData` polled from `requestAnimationFrame` is the simplest fallback (used by many tuner demos), but polling drops or duplicates audio when the tab is throttled and gives no sample-accurate timestamps, which matters for scoring against a timeline. `ScriptProcessorNode` is deprecated and runs on the main thread; do not start a new app on it.
- Use the context's native sample rate (44.1 or 48 kHz) for MPM and pass `audioContext.sampleRate` into the detector; never hard-code 44100. Only the SwiftF0 path needs 16 kHz, and the demo's resampler shows the fallback is needed in practice.
- React integration: keep `AudioContext`, stream, nodes and worker in a module-level singleton or `useRef`; write the pitch stream into a mutable ring buffer (or a Zustand store read via `subscribe`), and draw the scrolling graph on a `<canvas>` inside a `requestAnimationFrame` loop. Only commit low-rate state (current exercise, note results, session score) through `useState`/store updates. Nightingale's `setLatestPitch` at 30 Hz works but re-renders the subscriber 30 times a second.
- Canvas 2D is sufficient for one pitch trace plus target bars at 60 fps; SVG creates DOM churn at this rate, and WebGL is unnecessary for a single trace.
- Leaving `autoGainControl: true` (as the SwiftF0 demo does) helps models that are level-sensitive; for MPM, AGC off plus an RMS gate gives more predictable clarity values. Either way, check the applied settings.

### Gaps
- No authoritative, current table of which constraints each browser honours on each platform was found; behaviour must be tested on the user's actual laptop and phone.
- One search summary suggested that `{exact: false}` constraints can throw `OverconstrainedError` in Safari as well; this could not be traced to a source. Using plain booleans (or `ideal`) and inspecting `getSettings()` avoids depending on it.
- Because Safari may deliver stereo or left-only audio with echo cancellation off, the worklet should read channel 0 explicitly and the app should check for a silent channel; no source confirms the best handling.
- MDN pages for `AnalyserNode` and `ScriptProcessorNode` were not fetched in this session; statements about them are in Inferences.
- No measurements comparing AudioWorklet + Worker against AnalyserNode polling for pitch-tracking jitter were found.

## 4. Platform pitfalls (iOS Safari/PWA, Android Chrome, Bluetooth, laptop mics, speaker bleed)

### Takeaway
iOS is the main risk: starting mic capture changes the audio session, which has produced volume drops, earpiece routing and, on iOS 26-27, degraded or crackling output while the mic is live, with bugs still open in October 2026. Wired headphones plus the built-in mic is the reliable setup everywhere; Bluetooth headsets either drop to the low-quality hands-free profile or add latency, and playing reference tones through speakers contaminates detection.

### Cited Findings
- WebKit bug 218012 (opened 2020-10-21, closed "CONFIGURATION CHANGED", last comment 2026-05-20): Web Audio volume dropped sharply after mic permission was granted. WebKit engineer Youenn Fablet: "When starting capturing, we are changing of audio category which might explain the issue." Reported workarounds with mixed success: suspend/resume the AudioContext; route Web Audio through a `MediaStreamAudioDestinationNode`; set `navigator.audioSession.type = 'play-and-record'` before capture and `'playback'` after (worked on iOS 16.4 beta, "only sometimes" for others); disabling AGC/EC/NS helped one reporter on iPadOS 17 and did not help another on iOS 18.6. — [WebKit bug 218012](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=218012)
- WebKit bug 311451 (opened 2026-04-03, status NEW, last modified 2026-10-08): on iOS 26 (reported on 26.4, iPhone 15 Pro) output audio is degraded once `getUserMedia` activates the mic, described as reduced sample rate and stereo collapsed to mono, "with or without `{echoCancellation: false}`"; reported as persisting on iOS/macOS 27 betas, and on iOS 27 as audible crackling tracked in bug 326286, "REGRESSION (iOS 26.2 - 27): Audio output crackles while getUserMedia mic capture with echo cancellation is active". No WebKit engineer response and no workaround are recorded. — [WebKit bug 311451](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=311451); [WebKit bug 326286](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=326286)
- WebKit bug 282939 (opened 2024-11-11, NEW, last comment 2025-02-10; iOS 17.6.1 and 18.1): `getUserMedia({audio: true})` returned the built-in iPhone mic even with AirPods or wired headphones selected, and switched output to the iPhone speaker. A commenter's workaround: set `audioSession.type = 'auto'`, call `getUserMedia`, then set `'play-and-record'`; after closing the mic set `'playback'` then `'auto'`, because the session otherwise stays in play-and-record with degraded output quality. — [WebKit bug 282939](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=282939)
- The Audio Session API (`navigator.audioSession.type`: `auto`, `playback`, `transient`, `transient-solo`, `ambient`, `play-and-record`) is experimental and implemented only in Safari. — [MDN Audio Session API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Audio_Session_API); [W3C Audio Session draft](https://w3c.github.io/audio-session/)
- Mic permission in Home Screen web apps: WebKit bug 215884 reports recurring permission prompts in standalone mode when the URL hash changes; a later comment says prompts stopped appearing on every page in iOS 14.5 but permission still was not kept across sessions; in September 2024 a WebKit developer asked for a new bug to be filed if persistent camera/microphone access for Home Screen web apps was wanted. — [WebKit bug 215884](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=215884)
- Safari 18.4 (2025-03-31) made the Screen Wake Lock API work in Home Screen web apps and added a MediaSession capture-mute API. — [WebKit features in Safari 18.4](https://webkit.org/blog/16574/webkit-features-in-safari-18-4/)
- Bluetooth: opening a headset's microphone moves it from the stereo A2DP profile to the hands-free profile, which degrades playback as well as capture; selecting the device's built-in microphone as input keeps the headset in A2DP. — [Gcore docs](https://docs.gcore.com/streaming/faq/real-time-video-issues/audio-quality-is-reduced-when-using-bluetooth-headsets-in-video-call-apps)
- HFP audio is described as mono at 8 kHz (CVSD) or 16 kHz (mSBC) (secondary, low-authority troubleshooting page). — [fixmic AirPods page](https://fixmic.pages.dev/airpods-bluetooth-microphone)
- Speaker bleed: singing-training sites instruct users to wear headphones because "without headphones, your microphone picks up both the reference note and your voice, which confuses the pitch detection", and prefer wired over Bluetooth because of delay. — [musci.io singing test](https://musci.io/singing-test); [30 Day Singer guide](https://new.30daysinger.com/blog/how-to-tell-if-you-re-singing-in-tune-using-free-websites-apps-to-improve-your-pitch-accuracy)
- Some pitch monitors avoid the problem by not playing reference pitches at all (OnPitch). — [OnPitch App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1212118228)
- Nightingale handles speaker-to-mic delay with a calibration step that "plays a short beep, listens for it through the selected microphone" and stores a compensation value (default 0.08 s, range 0-0.5 s). — [Nightingale scoring docs](https://github.com/rzru/nightingale/blob/master/site/src/content/docs/docs/scoring.md); [constants.ts](https://github.com/rzru/nightingale/blob/master/client/src/features/playback/lib/pitch/constants.ts)
- Laptop microphones: manufacturers generally do not publish built-in mic specifications, and a vendor-forum reply describes built-in mics as "only designed for speech, not full or high frequency applications" (anecdotal; no measurements). — [HP support forum](https://h30434.www3.hp.com/t5/Notebook-Audio/Technical-characteristic-of-a-microphone/m-p/6053934); [Dell community thread](https://www.dell.com/community/Inspiron/Microphone-low-pass-filtration/m-p/7423084)
- Android Chrome: constraints for disabling processing have been unreliable across versions and devices (see section 3). — [discuss-webrtc thread](https://groups.google.com/g/discuss-webrtc/c/wTUknxGXRyY)

### Inferences
- iOS start-up sequence to implement and test: on a user tap, set `navigator.audioSession.type` if present, call `getUserMedia` with processing off, create/resume the AudioContext in the same gesture, then verify output level; on stop, stop the tracks and restore the session type. Expect behaviour to differ between iOS versions and treat reference-tone quality while recording on iPhone as degraded until tested on the user's device.
- Because iOS output quality drops while the mic is live, a "listen, then sing" (call-and-response) exercise flow, with the mic opened once and the reference tone ducked or stopped during the sung phase, is more robust on phones than continuous play-along. This also sidesteps speaker bleed for users without headphones.
- For speaker users, cheap mitigations are: gate scoring during reference playback plus a tail, lower reference volume, and discard frames whose detected pitch matches the reference with implausibly high clarity and steadiness. Leaving browser echo cancellation on removes bleed but also distorts sustained vowels and, on iOS 26.2-27, is associated with the crackle regression; it is not a good default for singing.
- Low-frequency roll-off in laptop/phone mics weakens the fundamental of a 65-100 Hz note, but periodicity-based methods (MPM, YIN) lock to the period of the harmonic series and still work without a strong fundamental. The practical risk is an octave-up error when the second harmonic dominates, which argues for an octave-continuity check and a per-user range prior (general DSP reasoning; not verified against a source in this session).
- Use path-based or in-memory routing in the installed iOS web app, since hash changes have triggered permission re-prompts there.
- Mic permission may need re-granting on each launch of the iOS Home Screen app; design the start screen around a single "Start practice" tap that requests it.

### Gaps
- Current (iOS 27) status of mic-permission persistence in Home Screen web apps was not confirmed; the newest evidence is from 2024.
- No measured frequency responses for MacBook or common laptop mics below 100 Hz were found (a Faber Acoustical measurement of the iPhone 16 Pro mic exists but was not read).
- No source documents how browsers choose between a Bluetooth headset mic and the built-in mic, or whether choosing the built-in `deviceId` reliably preserves A2DP in Chrome/Safari.
- No open-source web trainer was found that implements reference-tone subtraction or adaptive cancellation; "use headphones" is the only documented approach.
- Android Chrome specifics (sample rate, default processing in 2026) rest on older reports.

## 5. Reference audio and exercise playback

### Takeaway
`smplr` (1.0 stable since June 2026, MIT, sampled piano with AudioContext-time scheduling) or `Tone.js` (Transport, Sampler) both give accurate scheduling; `soundfont-player` is dead. Represent exercises as arrays of `{midi, startBeat, durationBeats}` generated from scale-degree or interval patterns and transposed with `tonal`, which keeps them key-agnostic and trivially scorable.

### Cited Findings
- smplr: 1.2.0 (2026-11-06); "First stable release" 1.0.0 on 2026-06-13; MIT; 29,975 weekly downloads; 328 stars; 1.17 MB unpacked. — [smplr on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/smplr); [smplr releases](https://github.com/danigb/smplr/releases)
- smplr instruments: Sampler, Soundfont, SplendidGrandPiano, ElectricPiano, DrumMachine, DrumAbuse, Mallet, Mellotron, Smolken (double bass), Versilian (partial), Soundfont2 (.sf2). `start({note, velocity, time, duration})` schedules against `audioContext.currentTime` and returns a stop function; `await piano.ready` for loading; `output.volume` 0-127; `onStart` fires up to the scheduler's lookahead window early ("200ms by default"); v1.2.0 added a `maxVoices` option. — [smplr README](https://github.com/danigb/smplr)
- smplr samples are hosted on GitHub Pages (smpldsnds), which rate-limits requests and "can be a problem" with hot reload; a `CacheStorage` helper caches fetched samples and "only works in secure environments that run over https". Its Soundfont instrument is "intended to be a modern replacement of soundfont-player". — [smplr README](https://github.com/danigb/smplr)
- soundfont-player: 0.12.0, last released 2020-01-27. webaudiofont: 3.0.4 (2022-06-28), GPL-3.0-or-later. — [soundfont-player](https://www.npmjs.com/package/soundfont-player); [webaudiofont](https://www.npmjs.com/package/webaudiofont)
- Tone.js: `latest` 15.1.22 (npm 2025-04-27; GitHub release note 2026-07-12: "Snapshotting the current release before i release another version soon"); `next` 15.5.67 published 2026-11-13; MIT; 309,602 weekly downloads; 14,902 stars; 5.4 MB unpacked. — [tone on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/tone); [Tone.js repo](https://github.com/Tonejs/Tone.js)

### Inferences
- For this app, raw Web Audio + smplr's `SplendidGrandPiano` (or `Soundfont`) covers reference notes, scales and interval drills with far less bundle and API surface than Tone.js; schedule each exercise's notes up front with absolute `time` values derived from one `audioContext.currentTime` anchor, and reuse the same anchor for the scoring timeline. Tone.js is worth adding only if tempo changes, looping Transport or synth voices become requirements. Both use the same underlying clock, so scheduling accuracy is equivalent.
- A piano-like sampled tone is easier for a beginner to match than a sine, and its harmonics survive small speakers at C2-G2 where a pure 65-100 Hz sine is nearly inaudible on a phone or laptop. Offer an "octave up" reference option for the lowest notes.
- Offline/PWA: sample fetches from a third-party GitHub Pages host are a failure point; self-host the needed samples (or precache them with the service worker) so the installed app works offline.
- Internal exercise model: `{id, name, pattern: scale-degree or semitone offsets, rhythm, tempo}` rendered to `{midi, start, duration}[]` for a chosen root; transposition is integer addition on MIDI numbers, with `tonal` used for names, scales and intervals. Import/export adapters (MIDI via @tonejs/midi, UltraStar txt, ABC via abcjs, MusicXML via OSMD) can sit on top; MusicXML/ABC are needed only if notation display is wanted.
- Range fitting: measure the user's comfortable low/high notes in onboarding, then choose roots so every generated note lies inside that range, stepping the root by semitone as in conventional warm-ups.

### Gaps
- No direct comparison of Tone.Sampler vs smplr timing or sample quality was found.
- Whether smplr supports pointing at self-hosted sample URLs for every instrument was not verified.
- iOS/Safari caveats for smplr are not documented in its README.

## 6. Scoring (how karaoke and training apps turn a pitch track into a score)

### Takeaway
Open-source karaoke games score coarsely: they quantise to semitones, fold octaves, apply a 0-2 semitone tolerance and award points per beat or per note segment hit. A training app for a beginner should keep the fine-grained data instead (per-note median cents error, time-in-tolerance, onset error, stability) and treat octave errors as a separately reported event, with a measured latency offset applied before comparing against targets.

### Cited Findings
- UltraStar Deluxe (GPL-2.0, pushed 2026-11-07): the detected tone is reduced to pitch class (`Tone := ToneAbs mod 12`) and shifted by octaves until within ±6 semitones of the target, so octave is ignored; a beat counts as hit when `Abs(target − sung) <= Range` with `Range := 2 − Difficulty` (Easy 2, Medium 1, Hard 0 semitones); rap notes are hit by any sound. — [USDX UNote.pas](https://github.com/UltraStar-Deluxe/USDX/blob/master/src/base/UNote.pas); [USDX URecord.pas](https://github.com/UltraStar-Deluxe/USDX/blob/master/src/base/URecord.pas)
- USDX points: `MAX_SONG_SCORE = 10000`, of which `MAX_SONG_LINE_BONUS = 1000`; per-hit points are `(9000 / track ScoreValue) × ScoreFactor`, with `ScoreFactor` = freestyle 0, normal 1, golden 2, rap 1, rap-golden 2. A volume threshold gates analysis before pitch detection. — [USDX UNote.pas](https://github.com/UltraStar-Deluxe/USDX/blob/master/src/base/UNote.pas); [USDX UMusic.pas](https://github.com/UltraStar-Deluxe/USDX/blob/master/src/base/UMusic.pas); [USDX URecord.pas](https://github.com/UltraStar-Deluxe/USDX/blob/master/src/base/URecord.pas)
- AllKaraoke (TypeScript/React): each frequency record is mapped to a semitone distance from the current note; consecutive records with the same distance extend a "player note", with a 100 ms singing-break tolerance (`SINGING_BREAK_TOLERANCE_MS = 100`) and start/end snapping to the note boundary; a player note is "perfect" when distance is 0 and its length is within 0.5 beat of the target; vibrato is detected on in-tune notes. Multipliers: star 2, normal 1, rapstar 0.5, perfect bonus 0.5, vibrato bonus 0.25, rap 0.25, freestyle 0.25. The scoring helpers have unit tests. — [append-frequency-to-player-notes.ts](https://github.com/Asvarox/allkaraoke/blob/master/src/modules/game-engine/game-state/helpers/append-frequency-to-player-notes.ts); [calculate-score.ts](https://github.com/Asvarox/allkaraoke/blob/master/src/modules/game-engine/game-state/helpers/calculate-score.ts)
- Nightingale: compares live mic pitch "against the reference vocal track" extracted by stem separation (less accurate without separation); constants include clarity threshold 0.4, RMS gate 0.005, smoothing 0.55, push interval 0.02 s, `SEMITONE_TOLERANCE = 6`, default mic latency compensation 0.08 s; results are star ratings from overall pitch accuracy. — [Nightingale scoring docs](https://github.com/rzru/nightingale/blob/master/site/src/content/docs/docs/scoring.md); [constants.ts](https://github.com/rzru/nightingale/blob/master/client/src/features/playback/lib/pitch/constants.ts)
- Research-benchmark convention: a frame is correct when voiced and within 50 cents of the reference; octave errors are counted separately as 1100-1300 cents off. — [pitch-benchmark README](https://github.com/lars76/pitch-benchmark); [BENCHMARK.md](https://github.com/lars76/pitch-benchmark/blob/main/BENCHMARK.md)
- Note segmentation in SwiftF0 0.3.0 uses a `pitch_hold_ms` parameter (default 80; higher gives fewer, longer notes) and fits one pitch per note. — [swift-f0 CHANGELOG](https://github.com/lars76/swift-f0/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)
- Latency data available to a web app: `AudioContext.outputLatency` "provides an estimate of audio hardware's output latency" (for example Bluetooth earbuds), supported in Chrome 102, Edge 102, Firefox 70 and Safari 18.4; `baseLatency` covers the browser's own processing and varies with `latencyHint`. — [web.dev audio output latency](https://web.dev/articles/audio-output-latency); [MDN AudioContext.baseLatency](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioContext/baseLatency)
- Reported latency values can be unreliable: a developer reports that the capture-side latency setting in Chrome "always returns 0.01 (10ms) even when I switch to a higher latency microphone" (secondary, via search summary; the same summary mentions implausible `outputLatency` values in Firefox). — [chromium-dev thread](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/chromium-dev/c/YCywOaFTSm8)
- AllKaraoke ships an input-lag calibration module (`src/modules/calibration/input-lag.tsx`). — [AllKaraoke repo](https://github.com/Asvarox/allkaraoke)

### Inferences
- Proposed scoring pipeline for a trainer: (1) frame track at about 50-100 Hz with `{t, hz, clarity, rms}`; (2) drop frames below an RMS gate or clarity threshold (start near pitchy clarity 0.8-0.9 for scoring, lower for display) and outside the user's plausible range; (3) convert to cents relative to the target; (4) median-filter over 3-5 frames to remove single-frame spikes; (5) per note, ignore the first 80-150 ms (onset/scoop) and compute median cents error, percentage of voiced frames within tolerance, onset delay and pitch standard deviation; (6) aggregate per exercise.
- Tolerances: karaoke uses 100-200 cents; the research convention is 50 cents. For a beginner, grade bands such as ≤25 cents (excellent), ≤50 (good), ≤100 (close) are more motivating than pass/fail, tightening as progress data accumulates.
- Octave handling: do not fold octaves in a training app. A bass singing an octave below a reference melody may be intended (offer an explicit "sing an octave down" setting that shifts the target), but an unintended octave jump is either a detector error or a real pitch-matching problem. Compute both raw and octave-folded error; if folded error is small and raw is about ±1200 cents, flag "right note, wrong octave" and, if it lasts only a few frames, treat it as a detector glitch.
- Latency compensation: total offset = output latency + acoustic path + input latency + half the analysis window. Estimate it with a one-off loopback calibration (play a click or beep, detect it in the mic stream), fall back to `outputLatency + baseLatency + window/2`, and allow a manual slider. Re-run calibration when the output device changes, especially for Bluetooth.
- Scoring on the recorded take after the exercise ends (with a 4096 window or SwiftF0) removes the real-time constraint and gives more stable numbers than the live cursor; show the live trace for feedback and the offline result for the score.
- Sustained-note metrics (drift in cents per second, vibrato rate/extent, stability) can reuse the same frame track and are more informative for a beginner than a single score.

### Gaps
- Vocaluxe and Performous scoring code were not inspected; SingStar-style commercial algorithms are not public.
- AllKaraoke's `calc-distance` implementation (whether it folds octaves) and its end-to-end doc were not retrievable (the doc file is a "todo").
- No published evidence on which tolerance bands or feedback metrics best help beginner singers was collected here (pedagogy is outside this scope).
- The exact meaning of Nightingale's `SEMITONE_TOLERANCE = 6` was not traced through its scoring code.

## 7. Recording and storage

### Takeaway
Feature-detect the recording format: WebM/Opus now works in Chrome, Firefox and Safari 18.4+, with MP4/AAC as the fallback for older Safari. Store takes and history locally with Dexie on IndexedDB, request persistent storage, install the app to the Home Screen on iOS (the documented trigger for persistence), and provide a manual export; cloud sync is optional for one user.

### Cited Findings
- Safari 18.4 (2025-03-31; iOS/iPadOS 18.4, macOS 15.4): MediaRecorder "supports creating WebM files using the Opus audio codec", can also produce fragmented MP4 and lossless ALAC or PCM audio; Ogg container support for Opus and Vorbis was added. — [WebKit features in Safari 18.4](https://webkit.org/blog/16574/webkit-features-in-safari-18-4/)
- Safari 14.1-18.3 record MP4 only (AAC audio); suggested detection order is `audio/webm;codecs=opus`, then `audio/mp4;codecs=mp4a.40.2` or `audio/mp4` (secondary, vendor guide). — [TestMu MediaRecorder guide](https://www.testmuai.com/web-technologies/mediarecorder/)
- Chrome defaults to WebM/Opus; MP4 recording arrived in Chrome 126 (June 2024) (secondary). Chrome's WebM output often lacks a Duration element and Cues index, which hurts seeking; MP4 from Chrome/Safari can also lack immediately available duration metadata. — [Addpipe: duration in WebM](https://blog.addpipe.com/duration-in-webm-videos-produced-by-chrome/); [Addpipe: duration in MP4](https://blog.addpipe.com/duration-in-mp4-files-produced-by-chrome-safari/)
- Helper libraries: fix-webm-duration 1.0.6 (2024-07-04, MIT, 737,743 weekly downloads); mediabunny 1.66.0 (2026-11-13, MPL-2.0, 7.1M weekly downloads, pure-TypeScript read/write/convert toolkit); extendable-media-recorder 9.2.40 (2026-08-22, MIT, 320,070 weekly downloads). — [fix-webm-duration](https://www.npmjs.com/package/fix-webm-duration); [mediabunny](https://www.npmjs.com/package/mediabunny); [extendable-media-recorder](https://www.npmjs.com/package/extendable-media-recorder)
- WebKit storage policy (Safari 17 / iOS 17 onwards): an origin may use up to 60% of disk in browser apps (15% in other apps), 80% overall; Home Screen web apps get the same quotas as the browser; data is evicted when the overall quota is exceeded, under storage pressure, or after a period without user interaction, in least-recently-used order; origins in persistent mode may be excluded. `navigator.storage.persist()` is granted by heuristics, the stated example being "whether the site is opened as a Home Screen Web App". Covered storage: localStorage, IndexedDB, Cache API, Service Worker, File System. — [WebKit: Updates to Storage Policy](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)
- Safari's ITP deletes script-writable storage (IndexedDB, service worker registrations, Cache API) after seven days of browser use without interaction with the site; Home Screen web apps keep their own counter and their first-party storage is treated as exempt (2020-2022 sources; no newer primary statement found). — [WebKit bug 237350](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237350); [The Register, 2020](https://www.theregister.com/2020/03/26/apple_relax_were_not_totally/); [M. Tsai summary of the WebKit post](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2020/03/26/safari-13-1-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-7-day-script-writeable-storage/)
- IndexedDB on Safari has a history of Blob-related bugs: "REGRESSION (Safari 17.x): IndexedDB Error preparing Blob/File data to be stored in private browsing", "Cannot store blobs in IndexedDB on iOS in Private Browsing mode", and "Safari corrupts already-rendered blobs after saving to IndexedDB". Dexie's Safari notes mention instability "when tabs wakes up from background" in Safari 15-18. — [WebKit bug 268037](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=268037); [WebKit bug 198278](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=198278); [WebKit bug 240216](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=240216); [Dexie: IndexedDB on Safari](https://dexie.org/docs/IndexedDB-on-Safari)
- Storing the bytes as an `ArrayBuffer` and rebuilding the Blob on read is a reported workaround for Safari Blob failures (secondary, Stack Overflow mirror). — [Stack Overflow answer mirror](https://coderapp.vercel.app/answer/46331687)
- OPFS on Safari: a WebKit bug is titled "WebKit does not implement `FileSystemFileHandle.createWritable()` and `FileSystemWritableFileStream`", leaving `createSyncAccessHandle()` inside a Worker as the write path; a later comment is reported to cite Safari 26 release notes as adding the writable-stream API (unconfirmed against the release notes). — [WebKit bug 254726](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=254726); [WebKit bug 231706](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=231706)
- Libraries: dexie 4.4.7 (2026-10-19, Apache-2.0, 3.2M weekly downloads, 14,689 stars); dexie-react-hooks 4.4.0 (2026-03-18); idb 8.0.4 (2026-10-06, ISC, 35.9M weekly downloads). — [dexie](https://www.npmjs.com/package/dexie); [dexie-react-hooks](https://www.npmjs.com/package/dexie-react-hooks); [idb](https://www.npmjs.com/package/idb)

### Inferences
- Recorder: `const mime = ['audio/webm;codecs=opus','audio/mp4;codecs=mp4a.40.2','audio/mp4'].find(MediaRecorder.isTypeSupported)`; store the MIME type with each take. Opus at 48-64 kbps mono is about 0.4-0.5 MB per minute, so years of daily short takes fit comfortably within quota.
- Record from the same `MediaStream` used for analysis, and also keep the frame-level pitch track for each take (small JSON or `Float32Array`), so playback, re-scoring and charts never need to decode audio.
- Schema sketch (Dexie): `sessions(id, startedAt, device)`, `attempts(id, sessionId, exerciseId, key, startedAt, metrics)`, `pitchTracks(attemptId, hop, data)`, `recordings(attemptId, mime, bytes)`, `settings`. Keep audio in its own table so history queries stay fast; store bytes as `ArrayBuffer` for Safari safety.
- Persistence plan: call `navigator.storage.persist()` after first meaningful use, show `navigator.storage.estimate()` in settings, tell the user to add the app to the Home Screen on iPhone, and offer retention rules (for example keep all metrics, keep audio for N days or starred takes).
- Laptop and phone will be separate origins' worth of data (same origin, different devices): without sync, history diverges. Options in increasing effort: manual export/import of a single JSON or zip file; periodic export to a user-chosen folder; an add-on sync service. For one user, metrics-only export/import is probably enough; syncing audio is rarely worth the complexity.
- OPFS offers no clear benefit over IndexedDB for short clips and has had a Worker-only write path on Safari; skip it unless takes become long.

### Gaps
- Dexie Cloud and `dexie-export-import` capabilities, pricing and current status were not researched.
- Chrome's MP4 MediaRecorder support and Safari 26's OPFS `createWritable` rest on secondary sources.
- Whether `navigator.storage.persist()` currently returns true in iOS Safari tabs (not installed) was not confirmed; one third-party guide claims it returns false.
- Actual iOS eviction behaviour for installed web apps under storage pressure has no primary documentation beyond the 2023 policy post.

## 8. App shell (framework, PWA, state, charts, testing) and recommended stack

### Takeaway
A Vite + React + TypeScript single-page app with `vite-plugin-pwa` is the mainstream fit for a client-only, audio-heavy, offline-capable tool; the comparable open-source apps inspected (AllKaraoke, Nightingale, the SwiftF0 demo) are all Vite SPAs and none uses Next.js. Test the DSP and scoring as pure functions with synthetic fixtures in Vitest, and cover the mic path with Playwright using Chrome's fake audio device.

### Cited Findings
- React's documentation for apps without a framework: "We recommend starting with Vite, Parcel, or Rsbuild"; these tools "start off with a client-only, single-page app (SPA)"; suggested routers are React Router and TanStack Router. — [react.dev: Build a React app from scratch](https://react.dev/learn/build-a-react-app-from-scratch)
- Current versions (npm, 2026-11-13): vite 8.3.8 (2026-11-11); react 19.3.1 (2026-10-14); next 16.4.6 (2026-11-09); vitest 5.1.0 (2026-11-02); vite-plugin-pwa 2.0.0 (2026-10-03; v1.3.0 on 2026-05-05 added Vite 8 peer support; 7.3M weekly downloads; MIT); serwist 9.5.13 and @serwist/vite 9.5.13 (2026-10-04; MIT; @serwist/vite 28,000 weekly downloads); zustand 5.1.0 (2026-11-06); jotai 3.0.2 (2026-11-09); recharts 3.10.1 (2026-07-25; MIT); uplot 1.6.32 (2025-03-14; MIT; repo pushed 2026-11-13). — [vite](https://www.npmjs.com/package/vite); [react](https://www.npmjs.com/package/react); [next](https://www.npmjs.com/package/next); [vitest](https://www.npmjs.com/package/vitest); [vite-plugin-pwa](https://www.npmjs.com/package/vite-plugin-pwa); [vite-plugin-pwa releases](https://github.com/vite-pwa/vite-plugin-pwa/releases); [@serwist/vite](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@serwist/vite); [zustand](https://www.npmjs.com/package/zustand); [jotai](https://www.npmjs.com/package/jotai); [recharts](https://www.npmjs.com/package/recharts); [uplot](https://www.npmjs.com/package/uplot)

### Inferences
- Vite SPA vs Next.js: the app has no server data, no SEO need, and depends on browser-only APIs (AudioWorklet, getUserMedia, IndexedDB, service worker). Next.js adds server rendering boundaries and `"use client"` handling around every audio module without a matching benefit; a static Vite build can be hosted anywhere and installed as a PWA.
- PWA: `vite-plugin-pwa` is the higher-adoption, Vite-native choice; Serwist is the alternative if finer service-worker control is wanted. Precache the app shell, the AudioWorklet module, the ONNX model/WASM (if used) and piano samples.
- State: Zustand (or Jotai) for settings, session and exercise state; plain module singletons and refs for the audio engine; Dexie's `useLiveQuery` for history. Avoid putting the pitch stream in React state.
- Charts: Recharts is the most familiar to AI coding assistants and adequate for daily/weekly progress points; uPlot is the better fit if dense time series (whole pitch tracks) are plotted. The live pitch graph should be hand-drawn canvas, not a chart library.
- Testing layers: (1) pure unit tests on `detectPitch(Float32Array, sampleRate)` with generated fixtures: sines at 65.4, 82.4, 98, 130.8 Hz; harmonic-rich tones with the fundamental attenuated by 20-30 dB (simulating laptop-mic roll-off); glides, vibrato, added noise; assert cents error and absence of octave jumps; (2) unit tests on the scoring functions with synthetic pitch tracks, as AllKaraoke does; (3) a handful of real recordings of the user's own low notes as regression fixtures; (4) one or two Playwright smoke tests with a fake-audio WAV to exercise the worklet, worker and UI together.
- **Recommended stack (synthesis of all sections):**
  - Shell: Vite 8 + React 19 + TypeScript, React Router or wouter, vite-plugin-pwa 2.
  - Capture: `getUserMedia` (processing off, plain booleans) → AudioWorklet ring buffer → Worker.
  - Live detection: pitchy (MPM), window 2048 (cursor) and 4096 (scoring) at native rate, hop 512-1024, band 55-700 Hz, clarity and RMS gates, 3-5 frame median, octave-continuity guard.
  - Optional second opinion: SwiftF0 ONNX (135 KB) on onnxruntime-web in a worker, run on the recorded take; adds about 4.6 MB of WASM, so lazy-load it.
  - Playback: Web Audio + smplr piano (self-hosted samples), exercises as MIDI note arrays, tonal for theory and transposition.
  - Scoring: per-note median cents error with onset skip, graded bands, separate octave flag, loopback latency calibration.
  - Storage: Dexie on IndexedDB (audio as ArrayBuffer), `storage.persist()`, JSON/zip export.
  - Recording: MediaRecorder with `isTypeSupported` fallback chain.
  - State/UI: Zustand, canvas pitch graph, Recharts or uPlot for progress.
  - Tests: Vitest unit tests on DSP/scoring, Playwright with fake audio.
- Main feasibility risks, in order: iOS audio-session behaviour while recording (open WebKit bugs), octave stability at 65-80 Hz on built-in mics, and speaker bleed without headphones. None blocks a laptop-with-headphones build; the phone experience needs device testing early.

### Gaps
- No survey data on Vite vs Next.js adoption for PWAs in 2026 was collected; the recommendation rests on React's docs and peer projects.
- vite-plugin-pwa 2.0 breaking changes were not reviewed in detail.
- No source was found describing audio-specific test strategies in depth beyond the examples cited.

## 9. Optional AI layer and song-melody data sources

### Takeaway
Audio-language models are currently unreliable at hearing pitch (best frontier model about 54% on PitchBench versus about 73-75% for DSP and neural pitch trackers), so an LLM should be given the app's extracted metrics as text and asked for explanation and practice suggestions, not asked to judge intonation from audio. For song practice, the legally clean routes are public-domain or CC0 melody collections and the user's own transcriptions; UltraStar txt files of commercial songs are copyrighted transcriptions that the official projects decline to distribute.

### Cited Findings

**AI coaching**
- PitchBench (arXiv 2605.26176; submitted 2026-05-25, revised 2026-11-07): 28 tasks on absolute and relative pitch perception for audio-language models; "specialized DSP and neural pitch baselines achieve mean scores of approximately 73.2% and 75.2%" while the strongest audio-language model scored about 53.9%; accuracy shifts sharply with sound source, note duration and notation format, and multiple-choice formats inflate weak models' scores by up to 38.7 points. — [PitchBench (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26176)
- Gemini API audio input: 32 tokens per second of audio (1 minute = 1,920 tokens); audio is "downsampled to 16 Kbps" and multi-channel audio is combined to one channel; up to 9.5 hours per prompt; inline requests limited to 20 MB; accepted formats include WAV, MP3, AAC, OGG, FLAC, M4A, Opus and WebM; listed capabilities are description, summarisation, Q&A, transcription, diarization, "emotion detection in speech and music" and timestamped segment analysis. Pitch accuracy or singing assessment is not mentioned. — [Gemini API audio docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/audio)
- Research prototypes exist: "LLaQo: Towards a Query-Based Coach in Expressive Music Performance Assessment" is an audio-language model aimed at performance dimensions from pitch accuracy to articulation, and "Generative Multi-modal Feedback for Singing Voice Synthesis Evaluation" generates critiques of singing and relies on an LLM judge for evaluation (both known here only from titles and a search summary). — [arXiv 2409.08795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08795); [arXiv 2512.02523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02523v1)

**Song and melody data**
- The UltraStar txt format is specified by the UltraStar Deluxe project; note lines carry type, start beat, length, pitch and syllable; rap notes ignore pitch; golden notes score double. — [usdx.eu format spec](https://usdx.eu/format); [UltraStar Play wiki: txt file format](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/UltraStar-Deluxe/Play/wiki/UltraStar-txt-File-Format)
- Copyright position of the projects themselves: "there is no official song service yet for UltraStar songs due to copyright issues"; any song, lyrics or score "may be copyright protected in your country"; community databases such as USDB distribute txt files only, without audio. A charting guide warns that adapting a song for personal use "does not mean you can share it with anyone". — [UltraStar Play wiki: Songs](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/UltraStar-Deluxe/Play/wiki/Songs); [Karedi guide](https://karedi.gitbook.io/karedi/creating-songs-for-beginners/before-you-start)
- AllKaraoke's repository carries 7,333 song `.txt` files (notes and lyrics, including commercial titles) under `public/songs/`; its `package.json` declares MIT but the repository has no licence file at its root. — [AllKaraoke repo](https://github.com/Asvarox/allkaraoke); [AllKaraoke package.json](https://github.com/Asvarox/allkaraoke/blob/master/package.json)
- Public-domain / CC0 symbolic collections: PDMX (large MusicXML set selected by public-domain mark or CC0 on MuseScore; a later release notes a licence-metadata discrepancy affecting 31,221 songs, 12.29%); MelodyHub (261,900 melodies in ABC, maintainers "believe" all are public domain); OpenEWLD (public-domain lead sheets in MusicXML from the Wikifonia archive; metadata partly CC BY-NC); alexsosn/abc-collection (English, Irish and Scottish folk tunes in ABC; per-file terms). — [PDMX](https://github.com/speechprojects/PDMX); [PDMX Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/15571083); [MelodyHub](https://huggingface.co/datasets/sander-wood/melodyhub); [OpenEWLD](https://github.com/00sapo/OpenEWLD); [abc-collection](https://github.com/alexsosn/abc-collection)
- Melody extraction from user audio: Nightingale separates vocals with UVR Karaoke or Demucs and scores against the extracted reference vocal, but its analyser is a local Python/ML process with a first-run download of "several gigabytes"; scoring against an unseparated mix is "less accurate". — [Nightingale README](https://github.com/rzru/nightingale); [Nightingale scoring docs](https://github.com/rzru/nightingale/blob/master/site/src/content/docs/docs/scoring.md)
- Basic Pitch (Apache-2.0) is polyphonic audio-to-MIDI with semitone-resolution notes; the JS package is 1.0.1 from 2022. — [@spotify/basic-pitch](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@spotify/basic-pitch); [BENCHMARK.md](https://github.com/lars76/pitch-benchmark/blob/main/BENCHMARK.md)

### Inferences
- What an LLM can add from extracted data: turning per-note cents errors, drift, onset timing, range usage and week-over-week trends into plain-language feedback and a next-exercise plan; spotting patterns such as "flat on descending intervals" or "sharp above A2". This costs a few hundred tokens of JSON per take and needs no audio upload.
- What requires audio and is weakly supported: breath noise, strain, vowel shape, resonance, register transitions and tone quality. General-purpose models downsample to a low bitrate and are not validated for these judgements; any such feedback should be labelled as tentative, and pitch claims from the model should be ignored in favour of the app's own measurements.
- Calling a model API from a client-only app exposes the API key in the browser; for a single user this is tolerable with a user-supplied key stored locally, otherwise a minimal serverless proxy is the one backend piece worth adding. Keep the feature optional so the app remains fully offline-capable.
- Song practice roadmap by effort: (1) built-in public-domain melodies stored as note arrays (folk songs, hymns, traditional tunes), transposed to the user's range; (2) import of UltraStar txt or MIDI files the user supplies for personal use, with an octave-shift control because most charts sit in tenor/soprano ranges; (3) melody extraction from the user's own audio. Step 3 in the browser needs vocal separation plus a monophonic tracker (SwiftF0 on a separated stem) and is a substantial project; Basic Pitch on a full mix will not isolate the vocal line.
- Legal position in short: melody and lyrics of in-copyright songs are protected regardless of file format; personal, non-distributed use of user-supplied files is the low-risk path, and the app itself should ship only public-domain or CC0 material, checking each item's status because bulk datasets have known licence-metadata errors.

### Gaps
- No evaluation was found of any specific commercial model (Gemini, GPT audio, Claude) as a singing coach; PitchBench's per-model results were not extracted.
- No browser-ready, permissively licensed vocal-separation model (Demucs/UVR on ONNX Runtime Web) was verified; feasibility and download size in-browser are unknown.
- OpenScore Lieder, the Essen Folksong Collection, Mutopia and IMSLP were not checked for licence terms or machine-readable melody availability.
- No legal source (statute or case law) was consulted; the copyright notes reflect the projects' own statements and general principles and are not legal advice.
- The LLaQo (arXiv 2409.08795) and singing-feedback (arXiv 2512.02523) papers were not read; only their titles were verified, and their results are not quoted.
