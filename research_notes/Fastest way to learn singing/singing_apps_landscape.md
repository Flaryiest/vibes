# Singing-learning apps and software tools: landscape as of November 2026

Research date: 2026-11-13. Method notes that apply throughout:

- App Store versions, update dates, rating averages and rating counts were pulled on 2026-11-13 from Apple's public lookup API (US store). Review quotes marked "App Store review" come from Apple's public "most recent reviews" feed (US store, up to 500 reviews per app). Written reviews skew far more negative than star-only ratings, so read them as a complaint inventory, not as a satisfaction measure.
- Source-quality warning: most 2026 "best singing app" roundups are published by vendors that rank themselves (Singing Carrots, Vocal Habit) or are desk research from store listings (AIToolsBakery). They are used below for feature and price facts and are labelled as vendor sources wherever they make judgements.
- Not retrieved: Reddit threads (r/singing etc. did not surface through the search tool; only second-hand quotes), YouTube vocal-coach review videos, and Google Play review text. See the Gaps subsections.

---

## 1. What are the main singing-learning apps and web tools today, and what does each do?

### Takeaway
The market splits into five families: gamified song/lesson apps (Simply Sing, Yousician), exercise trainers with a live pitch line (Erol Singer's Studio, Sing Sharp, Vocalista, SWIFTSCALES, Riyaz), plain pitch monitors (Vocal Pitch Monitor, Nail the Pitch), video courses with no automatic feedback (Singeo, 30 Day Singer), and a 2024-2026 wave of "AI coach" apps (Singing Carrots AI coach, Bloom Vocal, Singing Coach AI, VOIS, Rehear, Strivocal) that mostly have under 40 App Store ratings each. Vanido is dead and SingTrue has not been updated since 2016.

#### Product-by-product notes

**Simply Sing** (songs-first, gamified)
- Onboarding quiz on goals and favourite artists, then a voice test; songs grouped Basic, Easy, Medium, Hard, Extreme and unlocked with XP; daily warm-up videos, breathing tutorial, Recording Studio. — [American Songwriter, 2025-05-05](https://americansongwriter.com/simply-sing-review/)

- Range personalisation: the range test auto-transposes every song; user can test the highest and lowest parts of a song and shift the key manually. — [Sing.Salon, 2023-09-17](https://sing.salon/articles/tech-reviews/simply-sing-review-practice-singing-in-your-vocal-range-r41/)
- Pricing: $14.99-19.99/mo or $59.99-89.99/yr; App Store lists ten "Simply Sing Premium" IAPs from $4.99 to $89.99. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1592900217); [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/simply-sing-review/)

- Pricing: reviewed at $7.49/mo (Premium, no real songs), $11.66/mo (Premium+), $17.49/mo (Family), billed yearly, in May 2025; listed at $19.99/mo or $119.99/yr in Sept-Oct 2026. — [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/yousician-singing-review/); [AIToolsBakery, 2026-10-16](https://aitoolsbakery.com/?p=18815)

### Inferences
- The two products with real scale (Simply Sing at about 482K ratings, Yousician) are gamified song apps; the tools that voice-teacher and long-term user reviews treat as the most useful for actual training (Erol, SWIFTSCALES, pitch monitors) are small, old, and cheap. Scale follows songs and marketing, not training depth.
- The AI-coach category is too new and too thinly reviewed to copy with confidence; early reviews (see section 4) suggest LLM-generated critiques are the weakest part.
- For a personal web app, Singing Carrots (browser, range test, pitch game, statistics dashboard) and Erol (range-anchored progressive drills with stars) are the two closest functional references.

### Gaps
- Could not verify current status of VoCo Vocal Coach or the exact iOS/Android availability of Vocalizer.
- Perfect Ear's singing-specific features were not examined first-hand.
- Google Play ratings and review text were not pulled directly; Android figures are second-hand.

## 2. How is each curriculum structured, what real-time feedback is shown, how is scoring done, how do apps personalise to range, and how do they track progress?

### Takeaway
Nearly every product uses the same feedback primitive (a scrolling piano-roll of target bars with the sung pitch drawn over it) and scores only pitch and timing as a percentage or stars. Range personalisation is common in marketing but usually means a one-off low/high note test followed by transposition; longitudinal tracking beyond streaks and XP is rare (Singing Carrots' statistics page and Erol's practice-time log are the main exceptions).

### Inferences
- Scoring thresholds are opaque everywhere. No reviewed product publishes its cents tolerance, how it treats note onsets, or how octave errors are handled, so scores are not comparable across apps or even across versions (see the Singing Coach AI review in section 4 where an update lowered all scores).
- "Progress" in commercial apps is mostly engagement currency (XP, stars, streaks). Objective skill curves (median cents error per week, range in semitones over months) are shown only by Singing Carrots and are otherwise absent.
- The star-gated, four-difficulty exercise ladder in Erol is the clearest existing model of mastery-based progression for drills.

### Gaps
- Exact scoring formulas (tolerance windows, timing weights) for Yousician, Simply Sing, Sing Sharp and Smule are unpublished; I found no official documentation.
- Yousician's official singing set-up article returned HTTP 403, so its headphone, transposition and octave-handling guidance is unverified.

## 3. How well do these apps handle low male voices?

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin but consistent that low voices are the edge case: coarse range presets, range tests that misclassify, guide exercises that start too high (a bass unknowingly singing an octave below the target), and pitch trackers that jump octaves on low or creaky notes. Erol and SWIFTSCALES have the best low-voice user reports, and no review found specifically tests any major app with a bass.

### Cited Findings
- 30 Day Singer forum: a beginner found the "Baritone Warmup" exercises start on F3 while his pitch app showed he was singing F2, an octave below; "I noticed that my voice was way lower than expected." The instructor replied "it's very possible that you're more of a bass than baritone" and pointed him to a bass/baritone playlist, which "felt so much better". (Post is about 5 years old.) — [30 Day Singer forum](https://www.30daysinger.com/forums/discussion/singing-basics/Beginner-Baritone-Warmup)
- Sing Sharp, App Store review 2017-12-24: "You can adjust the range, but only between three settings... Each of the three range settings is almost two octaves apart so there's a good chance a particular [song] won't work for you." — [App Store reviews feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=772052329/sortby=mostrecent/json)
- Sing Sharp, 2017-11-24: "it doesn't really have songs for lower male voices, so I can't hit the notes on the songs." — [App Store reviews feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=772052329/sortby=mostrecent/json)

- Sing Whiz, 2026-06-04: "Vocal range exercises are incredibly limited, and didn't hit my high or low cap notes." — [App Store reviews feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1623180736/sortby=mostrecent/json)
- Technical background: plain autocorrelation pitch detection often misreports pitch by an octave; errors arise when the second harmonic or a subharmonic is strong (octave doubling or halving). — [Wikipedia: Pitch detection algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_detection_algorithm); [Maynooth thesis](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/14192/1/JT_an%20investigation.pdf)
- YIN reduces subharmonic errors via an absolute-threshold step (typically 0.10-0.15) and by taking the first qualifying dip; the search band matters, with about 80-250 Hz suggested for adult speaking voice. — [MetricGate YIN docs](https://metricgate.com/docs/yin-pitch-detector/)
- The most common way apps can harm a voice is to "push into notes you cannot yet reach". — [AIToolsBakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/?p=18815)

### Inferences
- A bass's lowest notes (around E2, roughly 82 Hz, and below) sit at or under the lower search bound many trackers use for speech, and phone microphones and creaky onsets make the fundamental weak, so octave-up misreads are the expected failure. A custom app should set the detector's minimum frequency well below the user's lowest note (for example 55-60 Hz), apply octave-jump smoothing, and require headphones so the guide tone does not leak into the mic.
- Octave-agnostic scoring (as in AllKaraoke) is the cheapest fix for the "guide is an octave too high" problem, but it hides whether the user is actually in the intended register; better to transpose the exercise into the detected range and display the true octave.
- Coarse voice-type buckets (three presets, or soprano/alto/tenor/baritone labels) are the root of most range complaints. Storing the user's actual lowest and highest comfortable notes and fitting every exercise inside that span, starting near speaking pitch as Erol does, avoids the classification problem entirely.
- For an 18-year-old male, range is still settling, so the app should re-test range regularly (Erol's developer suggests every couple of weeks) rather than fixing it at onboarding.

### Gaps
- No independent test of any major app's pitch detection accuracy on bass or low-baritone voices was found.
- Yousician's octave tolerance (whether singing an octave below a target counts) could not be confirmed; its support page was inaccessible.
- No Reddit threads on bass-specific app problems were retrievable.

### Inferences
- Users cannot tell whether they are improving; that is the common thread behind the Vanido quote, the opaque scores, and the "is the app wrong or am I wrong" detection complaints. A trustworthy, repeatable benchmark matters more to a beginner than a richer game.
- Demo-voice bias (scoring high only when mimicking the model singer's timing and octave) and timing misalignment are plausible causes of "trained singer scores 25%" reports; scoring should be tolerant of onset timing in drills and judge sustained pitch, not attack.
- Pitch scoring that rewards forcing a note into the bar can train tension. The teacher-approved pattern is to keep exercises in an easy range, build up gradually, and avoid pressuring live correction on sustained notes.
- LLM-written critiques currently add little for beginners; plain-language, rule-based feedback tied to measured data ("you were flat by about a third of a semitone on the top note in 4 of 5 tries") is likely more useful.

## 5. Is there independent or published evidence that any of these apps improves singing accuracy?

### Takeaway
No independent controlled trial of any current consumer singing app was found; the only app-specific outcome data is Singing Carrots' own uncontrolled product analytics. The research literature on real-time visual pitch feedback is mixed: some lab and classroom studies show gains (largest for beginners, and when feedback includes clear right/wrong information), while a 10-week randomised study with adult non-musicians found no advantage over control.

### Cited Findings
- Blanco, Tassani and Ramirez (2021, Frontiers in Psychology): 47 beginners with no music experience in three groups (real-time visual feedback with knowledge of results, equal-timbre auditory, control), single session; "only participants receiving real-time visual feedback learned and retained in the transfer phase the mapping"; voice improvement averaged about 105 cents for the feedback group versus none for the auditory-only group. — [Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full)
- Paney and Tharp (2019): university non-music majors randomised to play the SingingCoach game with concurrent visual feedback for 10 weeks; no significant difference from control; visual-feedback participants trended better but retention fell to control levels once feedback was removed; self-ratings of singing rose. The Frontiers authors note the control group also received knowledge of results, which limits interpretation. — [Frontiers in Psychology (as reviewed)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full); [scite author page (abstract summary)](https://scite.ai/authors/kevin-tharp-11lEWX)
- Welch, Howard and Rush (1989): accuracy dropped during feedback but "improved considerably in retention tests"; commonly cited as showing visual feedback beats control. — [Frontiers in Psychology (as reviewed)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full)
- Wilson et al. (2005): beginners gained more from real-time visual feedback than advanced singers, and performance tended to worsen at the moment feedback was given. — [Frontiers in Psychology (as reviewed)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full)
- Hutchins and Peretz (2012) and Welch (1984): visual feedback without knowledge of results did not improve pitch matching. — [Frontiers in Psychology (as reviewed)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full)
- Zaki-Azat (2016 thesis, Wilfrid Laurier): no overall effect of real-time visual feedback training on vocal control in either study (per search summary). — [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/143687707.pdf)
- Paney and Kay (2015): a concurrent-feedback computer game improved third-graders' pitch matching versus conventional instruction (cited by a vendor; not checked at source). — [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/do-ai-vocal-coaches-actually-work/)
- Hutchins and Peretz (2012): most poor singing stems from motor-coordination problems rather than hearing deficits; true congenital amusia affects only a small minority (Peretz and Vuvan 2017) (cited by a vendor). — [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/do-ai-vocal-coaches-actually-work/)
- A 2026 survey of 30+ years of automatic singing assessment names persistent problems: "the lack of standardized evaluation frameworks", difficulty separating voice from noise, and a gap between objective computational assessment and human evaluation. — [arXiv 2601.12153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12153)
- Singing Carrots vendor data (2025-11-26 to 2026-06-18): 2,073 singers, 13,206 sessions; paired four-week analysis of 358 singers showed +5.9 percentage points in-app pitch accuracy (78% improved); by starting band: below 75% (n=70) 65.7% to 82.2% (+16.5), 75-85% (n=98) +7.5, 85-92% (n=114) +2.9, 92%+ (n=76) -1.4. — [Singing Carrots 7-month analysis](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/ai-singing-coach-7-months-who-improves-most/)
- Same data, range: +2.8 semitones overall across 359 singers (62% expanded); under-18-semitone starters (n=61) went 14.5 to 22.0 semitones; 32+ semitone starters fell 2.7. — [Singing Carrots 7-month analysis](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/ai-singing-coach-7-months-who-improves-most/)
- Vendor-admitted limitations: no control group, self-selection, regression to the mean (the author says the true beginner gain is probably somewhat smaller than +16.5), no confidence intervals reported, range measurement is noisy (warm-up, fatigue, microphone, time of day), and "We have not measured transfer to unaided singing." — [Singing Carrots 7-month analysis](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/ai-singing-coach-7-months-who-improves-most/); [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/best-learn-to-sing-apps-2026/)
- AIToolsBakery, after desk research on nine apps, reports no test results for any app; accuracy claims are the vendors' own. — [AIToolsBakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/?p=18815)
- A consumer site's claim that visual feedback "can improve pitch accuracy by up to 90%" cites no study and should be disregarded (per search summary). — [Wiingy](https://wiingy.com/blog/best-apps-for-voice-lessons/)

### Inferences
- The defensible reading: visual pitch feedback with explicit right/wrong information helps true beginners learn the voice-to-pitch mapping quickly, but gains measured while watching the display may not transfer to singing without it. Singing Carrots' own caveat (no transfer measurement) and Paney and Tharp's retention result point the same way.
- Design implication: include feedback-off trials. A benchmark where the user sings targets with the display hidden, scored afterwards, measures the thing that matters and guards against display dependence. Fading the live line as accuracy rises follows the same logic.
- Regression to the mean and noisy range tests mean single measurements are unreliable; a personal tracker should use repeated standardised tests and rolling medians rather than one-off scores.
- Plateau above roughly 92% in-app accuracy suggests pitch-matching drills saturate; later progress needs different targets (range, sustain stability, song application).

### Gaps
- Could not access full texts of Paney and Tharp (2019), Welch et al. (1989) or Wilson et al.; findings are as summarised in the Frontiers paper and search snippets, and sample sizes and effect sizes are unverified.
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of Yousician, Simply Sing, Sing Sharp, Erol or Riyaz for singing outcomes was found; absence of evidence here reflects a limited search, not an exhaustive review.
- A 2026 ResearchGate paper describing a mobile singing-feedback app (82% of pilot users reporting improvement) surfaced but its sample and controls were not visible.

## 6. What features appear to drive retention and low friction?

### Takeaway
What users credit for sticking with practice: a clear "what's next", star-based mastery gating, short daily sets (3 exercises, 5-15 minutes), range-fitted material, and hands-free flow. What drives abandonment: long onboarding ending in a paywall, lives/energy systems that block practice, guilt-based streak notifications, headphone/Bluetooth failures, and error-toned prompts during the range test.

### Cited Findings
- Simply Sing's onboarding is 11 steps; the range test starts at about 2:43, "Adapting all songs to your vocal range" appears at 8:39 as a value-reinforcement moment, "Level 1 unlocked!" at 8:49, and the paywall at 8:57 with no free trial in the recorded flow. Messages such as "We can't hear you clearly" and "It sounds like you might go lower" read as errors and may discourage unconfident users. — [ScreensDesign teardown](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/simply-sing-my-singing-app)
- Simply Sing estimates: about 300-350K monthly downloads and $650-900K monthly revenue (two conflicting third-party figures on one page; unverified). — [ScreensDesign teardown](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/simply-sing-my-singing-app)
- Simply Sing offers "Short daily warm-ups of around five minutes"; ToneGym practice "takes 10 to 15 minutes a day". — [Vocal Habit (vendor)](https://vocalhabit.com/learn/singing-apps-compared)
- Vanido delivered exactly three new range-matched exercises per day. — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/vanido/about)
- 30 Day Singer structures beginners into 30 daily lessons of roughly 10-15 minutes with a lesson history to resume. — [Deviant Noise](https://deviantnoise.net/education/singing/30-day-singer-review/)
- Erol's retention mechanics: stars per exercise, locked sections (optional), trophies, an energy level that drains without practice, practice-time tracking, reminders, refresher indicators; a nine-month daily user credits "trying for max stars before moving on". — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id502780186); [App Store reviews feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=502780186/sortby=mostrecent/json)

A 2014 user asked for pausing without restarting ("don't restart a lesson if a phone call or text comes through"). — [App Store version history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id502780186); [App Store reviews feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=502780186/sortby=mostrecent/json)
- Vocal Ease 2 is praised for hands-free autoplay designed for use in a car; 7 Minute Vocal Warmup is criticised because users cannot pause, rewind or autoplay; Vocalizer is praised for rewind, speed control and gradual build-up. — [SingWell](https://singwell.eu/singing-apps/)
- Streak and notification backlash: American Songwriter's reviewer found Yousician's streaks and notifications manipulative; Kunstplaza notes some users say the streak concept relies more on guilt than genuine motivation. — [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/yousician-singing-review/); [Kunstplaza](https://www.kunstplaza.de/en/music/singing-lesson-app-singing-teacher-comparison/)
- Lives systems are resented: SingTrue users report being blocked until lives regenerate and pushed toward purchases. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id914239183)
- Riyaz's free tier reportedly caps practice at 8 minutes per day (per search summary; not confirmed on a primary page). — [Riyaz Play landing page](https://get.riyazapp.com)
- Singing Carrots vendor data: AI-coach users practised about 1.8 times more than self-guided users; singers whose sessions typically ran 20-35 exchanges completed 7.4 sessions on average versus 1.2 for those under 10 exchanges (correlational, self-selected). — [Singing Carrots 7-month analysis](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/ai-singing-coach-7-months-who-improves-most/)
- A Yousician user praised being able to slow songs down while learning. — [App Store reviews feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=959883039/sortby=mostrecent/json)
- Riyaz user, 2023-05-10: the app "requires you to put headphones on, which is an inconvenience." — [App Store reviews feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1341595277/sortby=mostrecent/json)

### Inferences
- Time-to-first-sung-note is the key friction number. Simply Sing takes about nine minutes to reach a paywall; a personal app with stored range and no account can open straight into "Start today's session" and be singing within seconds.
- A fixed daily set that auto-advances (warm-up, pitch drills, range work, cool-down) with pause/skip/repeat controls and no navigation between exercises matches what users praise in Vanido, Vocal Ease and Erol. For a 30-60 minute target, chaining several 5-10 minute blocks with rest prompts fits the short-block pattern the market has converged on.
- Mastery stars per exercise appear to motivate without the backlash that streak notifications draw. A streak can be shown passively (calendar heat-map) without guilt messaging or penalties.
- Headphone handling should be decided once: detect output route where possible, recommend wired headphones (Bluetooth latency and mic-mode switching are a category-wide failure), and offer a latency calibration step.
- Range-test copy should be framed as coaching ("try sliding a little lower"), not as error messages.

### Gaps
- No published retention figures (day-7, day-30) exist for any singing app; the retention drivers above are inferred from reviews and design teardowns, not measured.
- No evidence was found on optimal session length for adherence in singing apps specifically.

## 7. What clear gaps exist that a personal custom-built web app could fill?

### Takeaway
The gaps are structural rather than technical: commercial apps optimise for subscriptions and engagement, so they lack honest, repeatable benchmarks, transparent scoring, long-term objective trend lines, recording-over-time comparison, precise range fitting for low voices, and a zero-decision daily flow without paywalls. Singing Carrots covers part of this (free range and pitch tests, statistics history) but puts the daily plan behind a paid tier.

### Cited Findings
- Only one vendor publishes any outcome data, and it has "not measured transfer to unaided singing". — [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/best-learn-to-sing-apps-2026/)
- Longitudinal tracking exists at Singing Carrots (history of range measurements and pitch-training stats); elsewhere progress is XP, levels, stars, streaks or per-take scores. — [singingcarrots.com](https://singingcarrots.com/); [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/best-learn-to-sing-apps-2026/)
- Scoring is opaque and unstable: pass thresholds undisclosed (Simply Sing), accuracy method undisclosed (Singing Carrots), scores shifting after an update and failing a "goat voice" test (Singing Coach AI). — [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/simply-sing-review/); [Singing Carrots 7-month analysis](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/ai-singing-coach-7-months-who-improves-most/); [App Store reviews feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6752552523/sortby=mostrecent/json)
- Feedback is pitch (and timing) only: "It says nothing about tone, breath, resonance, or strain." — [Vocal Habit (vendor, about its own tool)](https://vocalhabit.com/learn/singing-apps-compared)

### Inferences
Candidate differentiators for a personal app, in rough priority order:
1. **Honest benchmark test** run weekly under fixed conditions: same target notes inside the stored range, display hidden while singing, reporting median absolute cents error, percent of notes within a stated tolerance (for example plus or minus 25 and 50 cents), and sustain stability. Published thresholds, never retuned silently.
2. **Longitudinal dashboard** of those benchmark numbers, comfortable range in semitones (rolling median of repeated tests because single range tests are noisy), minutes practised and exercise mastery; no XP.
3. **Fully automatic daily plan**: one "Start today's session" button, a fixed warm-up to cool-down sequence generated from stored range and recent weak spots, auto-advance, pause/repeat/skip, target 30-60 minutes in short blocks with rest prompts.
4. **Low-voice-first range fitting**: store actual lowest and highest comfortable notes, start exercises near speaking pitch, expand by a semitone only when recent accuracy is high, re-test range every two weeks, detector tuned down to about 55-60 Hz with octave-jump smoothing, true-octave display.
5. **Recording time-capsule**: same short phrase or song snippet recorded on a schedule (for example weekly), stored locally, with side-by-side playback of week 1 versus now. No commercial app found offers this as a first-class feature.
6. **Strain guardrails**: comfortable-range limits, explicit "stop if it hurts or you are hoarse" prompts at session start, self-rated effort after sessions (the app cannot hear tension, so ask).
7. **Plain-language feedback** generated from measurements rather than LLM critique; minimal jargon, with a glossary for a user who has no music background.
8. **No paywall, no account, local-first data** with export.

What a personal app still cannot do (and should say so): assess breath support, posture, jaw or throat tension, or tone quality reliably; a few lessons with a human teacher remain the documented complement.

### Gaps
- No market evidence exists on whether hidden-display benchmarks or recording comparisons improve adherence; these are reasoned from the research literature and complaint patterns.
- I did not survey desktop voice-analysis software (for example Sing and See, Estill Voiceprint, VoceVista) that teachers use; these may already offer some of the objective metrics listed.

### Inferences
- **Closest architectural reference for a React app:** AllKaraoke (React + TypeScript + Vite, in-browser detection through aubiojs, tested with Playwright). Its octave-agnostic distance-with-tolerance scoring and note data model are directly reusable ideas.
- **Closest feature reference for a trainer:** Singing Experience (range saved once and applied to every program, warm-ups stepping up by semitone, steadiness readout on recordings) under a permissive 0BSD licence, so code can be borrowed freely.
- **Licence caution:** aubio (GPL-3.0), pitchfinder (GPL-3 per package.json), Vocaluxe, USDX and MercuryPitch (AGPL-3.0) are copyleft. For a strictly personal, undistributed app this does not bite, but if the project might be published, prefer 0BSD/MIT pieces (pitchy, PitchDetect, Singing Experience, UltraStar Play).
- **Algorithm choice:** for a low male voice, a time-domain method with explicit minimum-frequency control and subharmonic guarding (YIN or McLeod family) is a safer default than naive autocorrelation; neural trackers (CREPE) are more robust but heavier in the browser.
- The UltraStar text format has a large community song catalogue and UltraSinger can generate files from any audio, which offers a path to song practice later without licensing a catalogue.
- The burst of zero-star single-file trainers in 2025-2026 suggests many individuals are building personal tools like this, but none has become a maintained reference; expect to assemble from parts.

### Gaps
- Algorithms used by each library (for example whether pitchy uses the McLeod Pitch Method) were not verified from source in this pass.
- Performous's actual licence text was not read (GitHub reports only "Other").
- AllKaraoke declares MIT in package.json but GitHub detects no licence file; confirm before reusing code.
- Low-frequency accuracy of the JavaScript libraries on real bass voices through laptop or phone microphones is untested here and should be validated empirically early in the build.
